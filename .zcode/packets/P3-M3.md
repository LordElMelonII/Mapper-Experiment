## PKG B-125 — Selection-driven panel resizes preserve the viewport (D81)

Mission: on the wide layout, a selection-driven panel resize (arc panel
open/close, panel swaps, the docked property panel, a future ST drawer) no
longer refits the map — the viewport is preserved (zoom and pan untouched,
zero compensation), with a pan-only selection rescue clamp keeping the
selected element inside the canvas and clear of interactive chrome — while
the narrow layout and environmental resizes keep today's refit byte-identical
behavior, pinned by desktop + phone e2e.

Read: `.zcode/agents/ui-editor.md` (your law).

Context (verbatim, with IDs):

- **D81 (register, EXECUTION-PLAN §1 R22 additions — the ruling this packet
  enacts):** "Canvas-container resizes split into two classes: an
  **environmental** resize (the browser viewport itself changed — window
  resize, orientation change) and a **panel-driven** resize (the container
  changed while the viewport did not — the arc/property panel columns, a
  future ST drawer). On the **narrow layout** (<700 px, the SCSS breakpoint)
  every resize keeps B-106's behavior — `cy.resize()` + fit + cull re-check;
  the blanket guarantee (everything fitted stays visible and tappable at any
  stage size) stands unchanged. On the **wide layout** an environmental
  resize keeps the same fit path, but a panel-driven resize **preserves the
  viewport**: zoom untouched, pan untouched … followed by a pan-only
  **selection rescue clamp** when the selection's rendered box would sit
  outside the canvas or under interactive chrome …: zoom never changes; no
  selection ⇒ zero motion. … The rule applies always (no pristine-view
  exemption) and to every panel-shaped container resize on the wide layout,
  view and editor surfaces alike (the floating property panel causes no
  resize); Phase 4 ST drawers inherit the preserve posture. Fit sites after
  D81: `mount()`, `selectLayer()`'s explicit fit, and environmental
  `notifyResize()`."

     rendered position is pixel-identical after the panel opens; only the
     lost band clips. Zero compensation IS the invariant's common case.
